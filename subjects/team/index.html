Understand the collaboration

Start with work people actually share

Begin with a situation, not a label about the team. The examples below are everyday illustrations, not assessments or customer case records.

A release crosses several roles

Design hands approved material to development; customer support needs the final changes before launch. If each person finishes their task but the release still stalls, examine the receiving point between tasks.

A shift ends before the work does

A service request passes to the next shift with an unresolved dependency. Ask what is known, what remains open, and who will acknowledge the handoff before the first shift leaves.

Follow one piece of shared work

Agree on the shared result

What must be delivered, for whom, and by when? Describe what a usable result looks like. Finishing individual tasks does not by itself show that the shared goal was reached.

Make responsibility visible

Who prepares the work, who can make the decision, and who accepts the result? Name the responsibility attached to each role rather than ranking people or inferring their abilities.

Confirm the handoff

State the input, expected output, receiving person, timing and unresolved risk. Ask the receiver to confirm what they can continue with; sending a message alone is not a completed handoff.

Review evidence of the outcome

Compare the actual delivery with the original goal. What was accepted, returned or still missing? Separate observations and records from assumptions about effort or intention.

Choose the next adjustment

Identify one unclear responsibility, input or checkpoint to improve next time. Discuss it with the people involved; this page does not score the team or save a shared work record.
