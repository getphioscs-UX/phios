Understand a service

Follow a need through to an actual service outcome

A school, clinic or community association must connect a person’s need to responsibilities, rules and resources that keep working when staff change. Consider one service at a time. This is a reading guide, not an institutional assessment.

Who needs the service?

Who is the service intended for, and who has difficulty reaching it? Distinguish the stated purpose from the needs people actually describe; do not infer a person’s eligibility from a country average.

Who carries responsibility?

Who accepts a request, decides it, and explains the decision? Identify the responsible role and a correction or complaint route, including what happens when the usual person is absent.

Which rules govern access?

Which published conditions, documents, consent and limits apply? Explain the basis for a decision and how an exception is handled; this page does not establish legal eligibility or grant accreditation.

How does a request move?

Trace enquiry, application, review, delivery and follow-up. At each step, identify what the person receives next and who can respond if the request stops moving.

What resources sustain it?

Consider staffing, time, facilities, funding and reliable information. Note constraints and service interruptions separately; national resources are background, not evidence that this institution can deliver.

What changed for the people served?

Separate activity counts, delivered services and outcomes. Use the institution’s own appropriately disclosed records and feedback with dates and scope; missing evidence stays unknown. Do not turn national indicators into a performance score.

1967: people, roles and the founding of ASEAN

One founding document lets us distinguish a person, a public role and an institutional arrangement. It does not tell us whether those arrangements later achieved their goals.

The event and its date

The ASEAN Declaration was signed in Bangkok on 8 August 1967. Indonesia registered it with the UN on 2 September 1983; registration is not the founding date.

Check UN treaty record 22341 →

Adam Malik: a signatory in an official role

Adam Malik signed for Indonesia as Presidium Minister for Political Affairs/Minister for Foreign Affairs. The signature establishes this role in this event, not sole authorship of the institution.
