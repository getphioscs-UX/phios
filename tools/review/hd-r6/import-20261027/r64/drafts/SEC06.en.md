### 6.1 The Three Connections Actually Present

Your completed Channels are 43–23, 29–46 and 37–40. Each exists because both endpoint Gates occur in this calculation. Other activated Gates do not become complete Channels merely because their themes appear related, and another person’s activation does not become part of your individual definition. This chapter considers what the three connected pairs make available as interpretive themes. The specific Sun, Earth and Line readings belong in the Gates and Lines chapter rather than being repeated as if every level said the same thing.

### 6.2 43–23: Making an Understanding Discussable

43–23 connects Ajna and Throat, inviting attention to the distance between understanding an idea and making it understandable to others. Your reasoning may have developed through experiences the listener has not shared. One practical approach is to state the problem, explain the essential relationship, offer an example and invite questions about what remains unclear. The usefulness of an insight depends on whether it helps address the actual issue. Fluency and conviction alone do not establish that the explanation is sound.

### 6.3 29–46: Learning Something You Can Carry Forward

29–46 joins Sacral and G. Participation, discovery and direction can be examined together: a trial reveals which conditions help and how far you want to become involved. To make the experience informative, define what you want to learn and when you will review it before you begin. Choosing not to continue does not make the experience worthless. It may have clarified the method, the resources required or the kind of participation you want to retain.

### 6.4 37–40: Support Through Workable Agreements

37–40 joins Solar Plexus and Ego. Care can be expressed through a clear agreement about who contributes what, when the work is due and how changed conditions will be handled. Supporting another person does not require an expanding responsibility without limits, and stating a limit need not mean a lack of care. Cooperation becomes more sustainable when both parties understand what they can expect and can discuss circumstances that no longer match the original arrangement.

### 6.5 Circuitry as Context, Not a Verdict

Traditional circuitry groups connections into Individual, Collective and Tribal families. These categories offer context for distinctions such as individual expression, sharing experience and mutual support; they do not establish an occupation or social destiny. This reading relies on the verified Center connections and the three completed Channels without treating an independently unverified channel-by-channel circuit label as a personal fact. Start by observing how expression, participation and agreements interact in one real situation, then decide which interpretations add something useful.
